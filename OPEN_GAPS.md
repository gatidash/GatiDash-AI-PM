# Open gaps

Things I could not substantiate, could not build without you, or deliberately
did not invent. Each one says what evidence would close it.

Ordered by what it would buy you.

---

## 1. One real, redacted artifact from the shipped system

**The gap.** Every checkable object on the site is something I wrote from
scratch. The four artifacts are labelled honestly — *reference implementation,
built for my own concept systems* — but that means the entire verification
surface belongs to systems that were never built. The one genuinely shipped
thing, five jurisdictions in production, has no artifact attached to it.

Reviewer 1 called this the single highest-leverage change available:

> *"One hour of redaction beats forty hours of reference implementation. The
> actual go/no-go gate list you used before that agentic layer went live. The
> real disagreement taxonomy with real counts, jurisdictions redacted to
> A/B/C/D/E. The one-page readout that got signed."*

**What closes it.** One page from the real thing, anonymised as hard as you need
— jurisdictions to letters, systems to roles, numbers scrubbed if they must be.
Labelled `Shipped` under your own key. Check your employer's policy first; if
nothing can leave, say so on the page, because *"I can't publish this and here
is why"* is itself more credible than four inventions.

## 2. The go/no-go call you made at the Business Advisory Group

**The gap.** *"For two years I sat on PayPal's Business Advisory Group, shaping
go/no-go calls on new launches"* is one sentence in About. It is the only claim
on the entire site describing authority over work that was not yours, and it has
no number, no example, and no case study.

Reviewer 1, round 1:

> *"That single anecdote is worth more to an L7 committee than all four
> artifacts combined, and it's currently a throwaway line."*

**What closes it.** One launch you held, or argued against, and what happened
next. How many launches went through the group. Whether you were ever overruled
and what the outcome was. I did not write this because I would have had to
invent the launch, the objection and the consequence.

## 3. Anything measured from outside PayPal

**The gap.** No user acquired, no customer found, no conversation with a buyer
you do not already work with. Both concept ventures are argued from third-party
market reports the pages themselves admit are unvalidated. This is the specific
reason the investor stopped at 7:

> *"A founder has to be able to answer 'how do you get the first customer' — a
> question this site never once asks itself."*

**What closes it.** The reviewer-agreement diagnostic, run free for three teams
in three different domains, kappas published anonymised. The investor costed it
at *"a week, not an hour, and it's the only thing on this list that would move
my answer from 'I'd place him' to 'I'd back him.'"* Or, more cheaply: one Head
of Compliance outside PayPal reacting to Meridian on the record — including the
objection, unanswered.

## 4. Denominators for the production numbers

**The gap.** `60% reduction in manual intervention` and `50% faster processing
turnaround` are on the résumé and stay on the site. Neither carries a baseline,
a window, or a denominator. Reviewer 1's opening screen question:

> *"You told me point estimates aren't evidence. Give me the n and the baseline
> on the 60%."*

The footer now concedes these are résumé point estimates held to a weaker
standard than your own evals. That is honest and it is still a soft spot.

**What closes it.** 60% of what, measured over what window, against what
baseline, on what share of total volume. Same for the 50%. If the counterfactual
is murky — how much of the gain came from the 2022 platform consolidation rather
than the agents — say so; the shipped case study already half-concedes it.

## 5. The "200+ colleagues trained" programme

**The gap.** It is on the site because you told me about it. It is **not on your
résumé**, and it appears in no other source I had access to. Both reviewers read
the headcount as an activity metric: it says who was in the room, not what
changed.

**What closes it.** Get it onto the résumé first. Then: what the CFO's return
was, adoption at 90 days versus week 2, who resisted and what changed their
mind. And the thing I asked for two turns ago and still do not have — **what you
told 200 people not to do with AI.** That single sentence turns training
delivery into governance, and it is the only version of this claim that connects
to the rest of the site.

## 8. Two things I chose not to do

**I did not write the "launch I said no to" case study**, though Reviewer 1 named
it the highest-impact change available. It requires a room, a disagreement, a
decision and a consequence, none of which I have. Inventing it would have broken
the one unbreakable rule.

**I did not invent a design partner, a buyer conversation, or a real merchant
baseline** for the concept ventures, for the same reason. The investor is right
that you are *"a product lead at a payments company with merchants, churn data
and CSM teams"* who used a benchmark instead of your own numbers — but I cannot
reach your numbers, and guessing them would be worse than the gap.
