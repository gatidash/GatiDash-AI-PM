Merchant Retention · 2024 generative → 2026 agentic

Not a smarter playbook. A retention agent that knows where to stop.

00
The honest starting point · 2024

The 2024 cut wasn't an agent. It was a generator wearing the word "playbook."

The 2024 version fed merchant signals to an LLM, had it classify a lifecycle stage, and had it write a tailored playbook. Useful as a demo — and the wrong altitude for the problem. It ran once, at the moment you asked. It took no action, remembered nothing, and never checked whether it worked. The output was a paragraph a human still had to act on.

→ View the archived 2024 v1 — the original generative playbook, kept as the honest before.

What changed · 2024 → 2026

Generation is a function: input in, text out. Agency is a loop — sense, decide, act through real tools, observe the result, adjust, with memory carried across turns. The 2026 rebuild moves the unit of value from "write the playbook" to "run the retention loop, accountably."

01
The real problem

Mature retention teams already personalize. What they don't do is close the loop.

Payment platforms and B2B SaaS lose 20–30% of merchants a year. The lazy diagnosis is "no personalization" — but most serious orgs already run propensity models and rules. The failure is orchestration: a risk signal fires and nobody acts on it; an intervention runs once and is never followed up; no memory of what was already tried; and no single owner accountable for the loop actually closing.

01

Signals with no hands

Risk is detected in a dashboard, then waits for a human who's busy. Detection isn't intervention.

02

One-shot outreach

A save email goes out once. Nobody watches whether volume recovered, so nobody adjusts.

03

No memory

The same tactic gets re-tried on a merchant it already failed on last quarter. The system never learned.

02
Design correction · 2024 → 2026

Put the right model on each job — and stop calling a text generator an agent.

An LLM is the wrong tool for lifecycle classification: that's a well-solved supervised-ML problem — cheaper, faster, and auditable without a non-deterministic model in the decision path. The LLM earns its seat where judgment and language matter: planning the next best action, generating the outreach, and orchestrating the tools. Classifier decides what stage; the agent decides what to do about it and does it.

2024 · v1 · Generative
  • Runs once, on request
  • Emits a document a human must action
  • No tools, no side effects
  • No memory between merchants or turns
  • Never observes the outcome
2026 · v2 · Agentic
  • Runs continuously, event-triggered
  • Executes through a governed tool layer
  • Carries memory of prior attempts
  • Watches results, then adjusts
  • Stops at a human gate on anything risky
03
The 2026 architecture

Five stages, on a loop, with memory — not a pipeline with an exit.

SENSE

Continuous signal intake

Volume deltas, ticket sentiment, feature-adoption stalls, plan-limit approach, failed payments — event-triggered, not a one-time snapshot at churn.

REASON + PLAN

Judgment over state and history

The agent reasons over the merchant's current state and its memory of past interventions, then selects a next-best action — skipping tactics that already failed here.

ACT

A governed tool layer

Draft & send outreach, open a CSM task, fire an in-app nudge, flag an upsell, hold a call, propose a retention offer, or escalate. It does things.

OBSERVE + ADJUST

Close the loop

Did volume recover? Did they re-engage? The outcome writes back to memory and reshapes the next decision. This is the single thing the 2024 version lacked.

04
Human-in-the-loop · the trust boundary

Autonomy is tiered by blast radius. The colour is the rule.

Every action the agent can take sits in one of three tiers. The tier is fixed by what the action can cost — margin, money, reputation, or an irreversible relationship — not by how confident the model feels.

Auto · agent acts

Reversible, no-margin, low blast radius

The agent executes on its own and logs it. Nothing here can cost you money or a relationship, and anything can be walked back.

e.g. in-app setup nudge · contextual feature tip · help-article surfacing · low-value templated check-in
Gate · human approves

Touches money, margin, or a key relationship

The agent drafts the action plus its rationale, expected impact, and confidence — then waits. A human approves, edits, or rejects in one click. Every rejection is training signal.

e.g. retention credit / fee waiver · discount offer · enterprise-merchant outreach · downgrade-save incentive
Human · owns the call

High-stakes, irreversible, or relationship-defining

The agent never acts here. It assembles the brief and hands it to a named owner. Surfacing, not deciding.

e.g. contract renegotiation · legal / compliance-sensitive moves · top-tier account above a value threshold

Seniority isn't maximizing autonomy. It's knowing exactly where autonomy ends.

06
Guardrails · always on

HITL is a gate a person stands at. Guardrails hold whether or not anyone is watching.

G1 · fatigue

Action budget & dedup

Hard cap on touches per merchant per window, with idempotency so the same intervention can't fire twice — and won't fire at all if a human is already engaged.

G2 · money

Spend ceiling

A physical cap on total retention credit per period and a per-merchant discount max. The agent cannot propose past it — the budget runs out first.

G3 · consent

Consent & policy

Respects DPDP consent and communication preferences, contacts only opted-in merchants, and constrains generated copy to pricing policy and brand tone.

G4 · doubt

Confidence threshold

Below a set classification or plan confidence, the agent escalates to a human instead of acting. Uncertainty routes up, it doesn't guess.

G5 · kill switch

Circuit breaker

If opt-outs, complaints, or churn tick up after agent actions, autonomous action auto-pauses and alerts. One switch halts everything.

G6 · accountability

Immutable audit log

Every signal, decision, rationale, action, and outcome is recorded. You can always answer "why did the agent do that?" — after the fact, on demand.

07
How you'd know it works

An agent you can't evaluate is an agent you can't ship.

E1

Backtest against real churn

Would this agent have flagged last year's churned merchants early enough to matter? Measure precision and recall on a labelled historical cohort before it touches anyone live.

E2

Offline review of proposed actions

Sample the agent's drafted actions and judge them — LLM-as-judge plus human spot-check — for policy, tone, and appropriateness, gated before anything sends.

E3

Online holdout

Agent-managed cohort vs. an untouched control. Primary metrics: merchant LTV and churn. Guardrail metrics: false-positive flag rate, escalation rate, spend vs. cap, opt-out rate.

E4

The override rate is the health check

Humans rejecting most gated proposals means the agent isn't good or isn't trusted. Humans rubber-stamping every one means the gate is theatre. You're steering for the honest middle.
